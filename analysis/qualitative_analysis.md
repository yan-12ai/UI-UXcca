# Qualitative analysis — Simplified Investment Interface

## Evidence base

This analysis uses the five paired comment transcripts stored in `Transcript/`. Each transcript covers the original Angel One website (Part A) and the redesigned Simplified Investment Interface (Part B). The paired audio files are stored in `Audio/`, but the text was not independently verified against the recordings during this analysis.

The themes below summarize what the supplied comments say. The evidence is exploratory and reflects five participants; it does not establish population-wide outcomes or objective performance changes.

## Themes and evidence

### P1 — Beginner orientation and navigation

**Original:** The participant saw many investment options and was unsure where to begin. NAV and expense ratio were not explained clearly enough, and the learning content took some exploration to find.

**Redesign:** The participant found Mutual Funds and Learn more directly and described the page as less crowded.

**Remaining issue:** Add plain-language explanations or information buttons beside unfamiliar financial terms, and provide a clear beginner starting point.

### P2 — SIP calculator and result interpretation

**Original:** The participant had to search through sections to find the SIP calculator, then found it difficult to separate their own contributions from estimated returns.

**Redesign:** The calculator was quicker to locate and its input layout felt clearer. Changing the monthly amount updated the result.

**Remaining issue:** Show the assumed return rate, invested amount, estimated earnings, and total projected value separately. Make clear that returns are estimates, not guarantees.

### P3 — Text size, contrast, and reading comfort

**Original:** The participant found some text small and used browser zoom, which increased scrolling. A dedicated display setting was not easy to locate.

**Redesign:** The participant used text-size controls and High-Contrast Mode and found the text more comfortable to read.

**Remaining issue:** Make the accessibility control more prominent and add brief descriptions for Reading Comfort and each setting. The participant was unsure exactly what Reading Comfort changed.

### P4 — Keyboard navigation and forms

**Original:** The participant had to move through many links using Tab and Shift+Tab and wanted a clearer focus indicator and an indication of how many account-opening steps remained.

**Redesign:** The flow seemed simpler and the focus outline was visible. The participant still found placeholder-only field instructions unhelpful and wanted permanent labels, a thicker focus outline, and a progress indicator.

**Remaining issue:** Use persistent labels, stronger visible keyboard focus, and progress information such as “Step 1 of 3.” This is feedback from a keyboard-navigation walkthrough, not a formal accessibility audit.

### P5 — Investment discovery and comparison

**Original:** The participant found many investment sections and thought comparing stocks and mutual funds would require switching between areas and reading a lot of information.

**Redesign:** The participant found the main categories clearer and liked the comparison feature and separate IPOs section.

**Remaining issue:** Explain risk levels and the differences between investment options more clearly for beginners. The participant also wanted a clearer explanation of what Reduced Motion changes.

## Cross-cutting findings

- **Reduced information overload:** Participants 1 and 5 specifically found the redesigned navigation less overwhelming; participant 4 also described moving through fewer unnecessary options.
- **Beginner support:** The Learn section helped participant 1, while participant 5 wanted clearer descriptions of risk and investment differences.
- **Accessibility controls:** Participant 3 found text-size and contrast controls useful, but wanted a more noticeable accessibility button and a clearer Reading Comfort explanation. Participant 5 was unsure about the practical effect of Reduced Motion.
- **Important information needs explanation:** Participants 1, 2, and 5 raised questions about terminology, estimated returns, or risk.
- **Forms and keyboard focus:** Participant 4 identified persistent labels, progress status, and stronger focus styling as areas to improve.

## Interpretation and limitations

The supplied comments support the conclusion that the redesign was perceived as simpler to navigate and more adjustable for reading. They do not contain measured completion times, click counts, or error rates. No independent transcript-to-audio verification is claimed. The sample comprises five participants and should be treated as exploratory. No questionnaire ratings or survey export were found in the repository, so there is no quantitative score comparison in this report.
