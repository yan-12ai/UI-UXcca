# Conclusion

## Overview

For our CCA project, we redesigned the Angel One website to make it simpler and easier to use, especially for people who are new to investing. We also added accessibility features for users who have difficulty reading smaller text or navigating websites.

To understand whether our changes actually helped, we conducted usability testing with five participants between the ages of 20 and 23. Each participant tried the original Angel One website and our redesigned version. We gave them tasks such as finding mutual funds, using the SIP calculator, comparing investments, navigating with a keyboard, and trying the accessibility settings.

After going through their feedback, we found that all five participants had a better experience with our redesign. They mainly liked the cleaner layout, simpler navigation, and accessibility options. However, they also pointed out a few things that we could improve.

## Problems with the Original Angel One Website

One of the main problems participants faced with the original Angel One website was that there was too much information on the screen. Although the website had many useful features, beginners found it difficult to figure out where to start.

Participant A, who had never invested before, struggled with terms like NAV and expense ratio. He was able to find the mutual funds section but felt that the website could guide beginners better.

Participant B had to spend some time finding the SIP calculator. Even after using it, she was confused about how the returns were calculated and wanted a clearer explanation of the results.

Participant C faced issues with smaller text. He tried zooming in through the browser, but that made scrolling more difficult. He also couldn't find an easy way to change the text size or contrast directly from the website.

Participant D tested the website using only the keyboard. She found it tiring to move through so many links and sometimes had difficulty seeing which element was selected.

Participant E felt that comparing investment options required too much effort because the information was spread across different sections.

Overall, the original website worked, but it seemed more suitable for people who already had some knowledge of investing.

## What Worked Well in Our Redesign

### Simpler Layout and Navigation

The biggest improvement participants noticed was the cleaner homepage. We reduced unnecessary information and arranged the main investment options more clearly.

Participants A and E said that the redesigned website felt less overwhelming. They could easily find sections like Stocks, Mutual Funds, IPOs, and Learn without searching through too many options.

Participant A also liked that he could move between the mutual funds and learning sections without getting confused. This was important because he wanted to understand investing before actually exploring investment options.

### Easier SIP Calculator

Participant B was able to find the SIP calculator much faster in our redesign.

She entered ₹2,000 as her monthly investment and selected a duration of five years. She also tried changing the investment amount and found that the calculator updated the results properly.

She liked the simpler layout because it was clear where to enter the values. However, she still wanted the calculator to explain the expected returns in more detail.

### Accessibility Features

One of the main things we focused on was making the website easier to use for people with visual difficulties.

We added features such as text-size adjustment, high-contrast mode, reduced motion, and reading comfort.

Participant C tried increasing the text size and said that it made reading much more comfortable. He also enabled high-contrast mode and noticed that the text became easier to see.

This was a major improvement compared to the original website, where he had to manually zoom in and out.

Participant E also tried the accessibility settings and liked the high-contrast option.

However, both participants felt that some accessibility features needed clearer explanations.

### Better Keyboard Navigation

Participant D tested our redesigned website without using a mouse.

She found it easier to navigate because there were fewer unnecessary options to move through. She was able to reach the login and account-opening pages using the keyboard.

The focus outline was also visible, which helped her understand which button or field was currently selected.

She did mention that the outline could be slightly thicker to make it easier to notice.

### Investment Comparison and Learning

Participants A and E appreciated having separate learning and comparison sections.

Participant A felt that the Learn section was useful for someone who was completely new to investing.

Participant E liked the investment comparison feature because it allowed him to understand different investment options without constantly switching between pages.

Both felt that these features made the website more beginner-friendly.

## Problems We Found in Our Redesign

Even though the overall feedback was positive, the participants pointed out some issues that we had not fully considered.

### Financial Terms Were Still Confusing

Participants A and E mentioned that some financial terms were still difficult to understand.

Words like NAV, expense ratio, and investment risk might be familiar to experienced investors, but beginners may not know what they mean.

Participant A suggested adding small information icons beside these terms so users could quickly check their meanings.

### Calculator Results Needed More Explanation

Participant B liked the SIP calculator but wanted to know which return percentage was being used.

She also suggested showing the invested amount and estimated earnings separately.

This would help users understand that the final amount is only an estimate and not a guaranteed return.

### Accessibility Options Could Be Clearer

Participant C felt that the accessibility button should be bigger so that users with visual difficulties could notice it more easily.

He also tried Reading Comfort but wasn't sure what had changed.

Participant E had a similar experience with Reduced Motion. He enabled it but couldn't immediately tell what difference it made.

We realized that simply adding accessibility options is not enough. Users should also understand what each option does.

### Form Labels and Keyboard Focus

Participant D noticed that some form fields used placeholder text instead of permanent labels.

Once the user starts typing, the placeholder disappears, which can make it difficult to remember what the field is asking for.

She also suggested adding a progress indicator to the account-opening process, such as "Step 1 of 3."

Another suggestion was to make the keyboard focus outline more visible.

## Recommendations

Based on the feedback from our five participants, we identified a few improvements that would make our website better.

1. Add simple explanations or information icons for financial terms that beginners might not understand.
2. Improve the SIP calculator by clearly showing the invested amount, estimated returns, and assumed return percentage.
3. Make the accessibility button bigger and easier to notice.
4. Add short descriptions explaining what Reading Comfort and Reduced Motion do.
5. Use permanent labels above form fields instead of relying only on placeholder text.
6. Add a progress indicator to the account-opening process.
7. Make keyboard focus outlines thicker and easier to see.
8. Explain investment risk levels more clearly in the comparison section.

These changes would help us improve the website without having to redesign it completely.

## Final Remarks

Overall, we feel that our redesign achieved what we initially wanted. Our main goal was to make the Angel One website simpler for beginners while also making it more accessible to users with visual difficulties.

From the usability testing, we found that all five participants preferred our redesigned version over the original. They found the navigation easier, the layout cleaner, and the investment information more organized.

The accessibility features were also an important part of our project. Participant C's feedback especially showed us how useful something as simple as changing the text size can be. Features like high contrast and keyboard navigation can make a noticeable difference in how comfortably someone uses a website.

At the same time, testing helped us notice problems that we had overlooked during development. We realized that some financial terms were still confusing, a few accessibility settings needed better explanations, and certain form elements could be improved.

One thing we learned from this project is that making a website look cleaner does not automatically make everything easier to understand. Small details like readable text, clear labels, visible focus indicators, and proper explanations matter just as much as the overall design.

In the end, our redesigned Simplified Investment Interface provides a better starting point for new investors compared to the original Angel One website. With a few more improvements based on the participants' feedback, we believe it could become even easier to use and more accessible to a wider range of users.
